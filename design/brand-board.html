Property NI — brand & landing page direction

Three logo concepts, three colour directions, a typography pairing, and a structural wireframe for making the page feel bespoke rather than template. Everything here is grounded in what the portal actually does: one entry point, role-based workspaces, multi-tenant operations across Northern Ireland.

Logo concepts

All three keep the existing navy + amber palette so nothing else on the site has to change. Each is drawn as line work with a single amber accent — they scale from favicon to hero and read at small sizes.

Recommended

A — The Doorway

An arched doorway with a keyhole: “one portal, one entry point.” The arch reads as property/doorway without being literal; the amber keyhole is the single accent. Strongest narrative fit for the whole product story.

Option

B — Roofline P

The letter P built from a house gable — property made literal, with the amber dot as the entry point at the apex. Compact and confident; works well as a standalone app icon.

Option

C — NI Monogram

Two verticals (the N and I of the region, two parties — landlord & tenant) joined by one amber diagonal: the platform that connects them. The most abstract; best if you want a mark over a wordmark.

Typography

The page currently renders in the system font stack (Inter is declared but not visibly loading). Pair a characterful display face for headings with a neutral body face — this alone does most of the “custom, not template” work.

Sora — display
Headings & hero. Geometric, modern, slightly technical. Distinct from the ubiquitous Inter/Roboto default.
Space Grotesk — display alt
A touch more personality and a monospace-influenced feel; pairs well with the “portal/system” subject matter.
Inter — body
Body copy, UI labels, forms. Keep Inter for the working text; only swap the display layer.

Load via next/font (already in use) so there is no layout shift. Display face at 600–700 with tight letter-spacing (-0.02em); body at 400/500.

Making the page feel custom (structural refactor)

The current page is a clean vertical stack — hero, screenshot list, onboarding steps, contact. That’s the template shape. The changes below break that rhythm so it reads as designed for this product, while keeping every section that communicates the portal’s purpose.

1 · Hero — split, not stackedLeft: headline + one-sentence value prop + primary CTA. Right: a live-feeling product frame (the login screen) tilted slightly with the amber accent bar, so the first thing seen is the actual portal, not a wall of text.
2 · “Who it’s for” role stripA horizontal band naming the three audiences — Property managers · Landlords · Tenants — each with a one-line outcome. This instantly communicates purpose and lets visitors self-identify. (New; currently implied only.)
3 · Inside the portal — asymmetric grid, not alternating rowsReplace the 9 long alternating image/text rows with a bento: one large featured screen (admin dashboard) + smaller tiles for calendar, org chart, notifications, map. Fewer scroll cycles, more visual variety, still shows every real screen.

4 · Onboarding — numbered timeline, connectedKeep the 4 steps but render as a connected horizontal timeline with the amber line running through the numbers, so it reads as a journey rather than four separate cards.
5 · Contact — full-bleed CTA bandPull the contact form out of the grey section into a bold navy full-width band with the form in a white card on top. High contrast, feels like a designed closing moment, not a form dump.

Smaller moves that add up

  • Add the new logo to the nav and hero (replacing the current placeholder PNG), with an SVG favicon.
  • Give each bento tile a subtle hover lift + the amber accent on focus, so screenshots feel interactive.
  • Use the display font only for h1/h2/section titles; keep body in Inter — instant “designed” signal with minimal change.
  • Add a thin amber top-border to the hero and footer to bookend the page in brand colour.
  • Replace the three-dot browser chrome on screenshot frames with the real portal’s top bar (navy + logo) for authenticity.
